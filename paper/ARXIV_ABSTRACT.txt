We study stochastic predictive embedding learning with one shared encoder and no exponential-moving-average (EMA) target network. Our model, Shared-Var, adds AdaSSL's low-dimensional Gaussian latent variable (the residual) to a LeWorldModel-style predictor, where the SIGReg regularizer, not an EMA target, prevents collapse. It trains with three terms: latent prediction, posterior-prior KL divergence and SIGReg. We compare it with a deterministic shared model, Shared-Det, and with EMA-target adaptations of BYOL and AdaSSL, in five paired replications per model on two synthetic tasks. A ridge readout maps forecasts to physical coordinates, where the Energy Score evaluates them. On Moving-MNIST, Shared-Var scores lower than Shared-Det in all five replications (mean paired difference -0.923 pixels/frame, 95% interval [-1.064, -0.782]), although much of this gap is already present when real future embeddings are decoded. Its difference from the AdaSSL reference is uncertain (the paired interval spans zero). Prior sampling beats the prior mean, and its projector features classify digits less accurately than those of the other three models. On an action-conditioned MPI3D task with held-out attribute combinations, none of the ten variational-model runs beats its own no-change (persistence) forecast on a secondary latent score that bypasses the readout; the deterministic models cannot by construction. A post hoc cost analysis finds that Shared-Var stores 37-42% fewer parameters than the AdaSSL reference, because it keeps no EMA copy. It needs 28% more training FLOPs per update on Moving-MNIST, where the posterior encodes a separate clip, and 14% fewer on MPI3D, where it reuses the target encoding.
