\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Predictive embedding models learn by predicting the representation of a target, such as another view, a masked region or a future observation, from an observed input~\citep{grill2020byol,assran2023ijepa,maes2026lewm}. When the input is compatible with several futures, a deterministic predictor must still commit to a single output. Conditional latent-variable models instead infer a latent from the realized future during training and sample a learned prior at inference~\citep{sohn2015cvae,denton2018svg}. AdaSSL~\citep{zhang2026adassl} applies this approach in embedding space: in its variational form, a low-dimensional Gaussian latent variable, which we call the residual, enters the predictor, and a KL term matches its posterior to a conditional prior. Its video recipe uses a BYOL-style exponential-moving-average (EMA) target encoder with a stop-gradient.

LeWorldModel~\citep{maes2026lewm} trains a deterministic predictor with one encoder shared by source and target. SIGReg (sketched isotropic Gaussian regularization), the distributional regularizer from LeJEPA~\citep{balestriero2025lejepa}, replaces the EMA/stop-gradient asymmetry as the mechanism against representational collapse. We combine this shared recipe with AdaSSL's Gaussian residual. The resulting model, \shvar{}, has one encoder and three loss terms: latent prediction, posterior--prior KL divergence and SIGReg (Eq.~\eqref{eq:s1}). Relative to AdaSSL's recipe, it removes the EMA target copy and its momentum schedule, and adds SIGReg with its coefficient and estimator settings. Whether this lowers training compute is an empirical question (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).

Two contrasts were prespecified: does the residual improve the shared recipe (\shvar{} versus \shdet{}), and how does \shvar{} compare with an EMA model that uses the same residual (\shvar{} versus the \emavar{})? To answer them we train four models, the \emadet{}, the \emavar{}, \shdet{} and \shvar{}, which cross two target recipes (EMA or shared) with two prediction families (deterministic or Gaussian residual). Each model is trained in five paired replications on each of two synthetic tasks, giving 40 runs. Embeddings have no physical units, so a ridge readout fitted on real target features maps each model's predicted embeddings to velocity or object position, and the Energy Score (lower is better) evaluates the resulting forecast distribution. A persistence control forecasts no change. The primary metric and analysis were frozen before main training.

We centre the presentation on Moving-MNIST, where an uncertain velocity change gives a continuous one-to-many prediction task. There, \shvar{} improves the forecasting pipeline over \shdet{} in all five runs and beats a source-pixel constant-velocity forecast in four. Prior sampling improves on the prior mean in every run, while much of the gap to \shdet{} is already present when real future embeddings are decoded. Its difference from the \emavar{} remains uncertain, and its projector retains less digit identity than the \emavar{}'s. The second task, built from MPI3D, is action-conditioned: a command either fails or succeeds, and test objects have attribute combinations unseen in training. On this task, no run of any model beats its own persistence forecast on a decoder-independent latent score. Section~\ref{sec:mpi3d} summarizes this result, and Appendix~\ref{app:mpi3d-results} gives the full analysis.

Our contributions are the shared-encoder Gaussian residual construction and a matched empirical assessment of its forecasts, its representations and, post hoc, its training cost. \shvar{} stores fewer parameters than the \emavar{} on both tasks. Its training compute per update is higher on Moving-MNIST, where the posterior observes a separate clip, and lower on MPI3D, where the posterior reuses the target encoding. Together, the results show that the construction trains without an EMA target and improves on its deterministic counterpart on one synthetic video task; on the action-conditioned task, no model we trained, \shvar{} included, beats persistence.
