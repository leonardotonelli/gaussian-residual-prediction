\begin{abstract}
We study stochastic predictive embedding learning with one shared encoder and no exponential-moving-average (EMA) target network. Our model, \shvar{}, adds AdaSSL's low-dimensional Gaussian latent variable (the residual) to a LeWorldModel-style predictor, where the SIGReg regularizer, not an EMA target, prevents collapse. It trains with three terms: latent prediction, posterior--prior KL divergence and SIGReg. We compare it with a deterministic shared model, \shdet{}, and with EMA-target adaptations of BYOL and AdaSSL, in five paired replications per model on two synthetic tasks. A ridge readout maps forecasts to physical coordinates, where the Energy Score evaluates them. On Moving-MNIST, \shvar{} scores lower than \shdet{} in all five replications (mean paired difference $\VMMSOneSZeroDiff$ pixels/frame, 95\% interval $[\VMMSOneSZeroLo,\VMMSOneSZeroHi]$), although much of this gap is already present when real future embeddings are decoded. Its difference from the \emavar{} is uncertain (the paired interval spans zero). Prior sampling beats the prior mean, and its projector features classify digits less accurately than those of the other three models. On an action-conditioned MPI3D task with held-out attribute combinations, none of the ten variational-model runs beats its own no-change (persistence) forecast on a secondary latent score that bypasses the readout; the deterministic models cannot by construction. A post hoc cost analysis finds that \shvar{} stores \VMMCostParamsVsROne--\VMPCostParamsVsROne\% fewer parameters than the \emavar{}, because it keeps no EMA copy. It needs \VMMCostFlopsVsROne\% more training FLOPs per update on Moving-MNIST, where the posterior encodes a separate clip, and \VMPCostFlopsVsROne\% fewer on MPI3D, where it reuses the target encoding.
\end{abstract}
